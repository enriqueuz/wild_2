Goal

Make the owner recognize their current situation.

Most restaurants know their regulars. They just can't reach them.

Body

For example:

Someone comes in for the first time.
You serve them.
They have a great experience.
They leave.

And unless they follow you on Instagram, remember your restaurant, or happen to see your ad again, you have no way to reach them.

You paid to get that customer once.
Then you start from zero again.

Then three pain cards
Every visit starts from scratch

You have traffic, but very little of it becomes an audience you own.

Social media isn't a customer list

Followers don't give you a reliable way to reach the people who actually eat at your restaurant.

Discounts without tracking are guesswork

They're useful for getting people in. This is for getting them back.

That creates a complementary category rather than positioning the product as another replacement system.

13. FAQ

This should handle objections rather than explain every feature.

H2

Questions restaurant owners usually ask

“Do I need to replace my POS?”

No. The system is designed to work alongside your existing POS and restaurant tools.

“What exactly does the guest see?”

They scan your QR code, access your digital menu and can choose to join your restaurant's text list.

“Do guests have to download an app?”

No. The experience is designed around the phone and SMS.

“Can I see which promotions actually worked?”

Yes. Promotion codes let you track campaign-driven sales.

“How do customers unsubscribe?”

STOP/HELP handling and the required SMS compliance controls are built into the system.

“How long does setup take?”

The restaurant's platform and menu can be prepared within a few business days; SMS activation takes longer because of carrier verification.

The internal proposal currently communicates 2–3 business days for platform/menu setup and up to 7 additional business days for SMS activation.

“Can I cancel?”

Answer transparently according to the final commercial terms.

“Do you write the campaigns for us?”

This is particularly important because campaign writing is currently outside the base plan.

One major strategic recommendation

I would not lead with the phrase “loyalty program.”

The internal proposal currently calls it a “Sistema de Lealtad,” but the deeper research correctly identifies the problem: customers increasingly expect points, tiers, rewards and other loyalty mechanics when they hear “loyalty.” This product doesn't currently have those mechanics.

So the landing page should make this distinction very clear:

Not another points program.
A simple way to own your guest list and bring people back.

That is a considerably sharper offer.

And the single most important line on the entire page should probably be:

Turn walk-in guests into a customer list you can reach again.

It captures the actual product, the pain, the differentiator and the desired outcome in one sentence.

The landing page should ultimately make the restaurant owner think: “I don't need another complicated restaurant platform. I just need a way to stop losing contact with the people who already came in.”
